## Claim status vocabulary

- `verified`: direct, reproducible evidence supports the narrowly worded claim.
- `vendor_claim`: the company says this; it is not an independent test.
- `user_allegation`: an individual reported an experience; it is not a finding.
- `unverified`: evidence is missing, ambiguous or stale.
- `superseded`: a later dated source replaces this observation.

Every comparison entry should carry claim, subject, state, primary source URL,
observed date, event date when relevant, response URL when available, and a short
scope note. Distinguish product code being public from the complete hosted service
being open source. Do not state that ISPATLA matches SuperX feature for feature.

example: “A growth promise, a cancellation button users say was hard to find—two
user accounts, not an established finding.” It must not turn those reports into
an accusation of fraud or a general statement about the company.

If an evidence dossier is published, include the claimant/source, date, company
response if available, evidence state, last checked date, independent source links
and a correction channel. Omit personal details, speculation and unverified
accusations. Invite a factual correction and preserve dated revisions.

## Editorial position

SuperX may better suit users seeking a managed, broad growth suite. ISPATLA is for
people who want to inspect and shape their own workflow. The distinction is
control and evidence, not an unsupported claim that competitors are dishonest.
